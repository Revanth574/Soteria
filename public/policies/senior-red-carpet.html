Several independent caps apply to the same claim, all at once.

This senior-citizen policy layers multiple cost-sharing mechanisms on top of each other — a mandatory co-pay, category-specific caps on what it pays doctors versus hospitals, and hard rupee ceilings on major-disease treatment — and none of them cancel each other out. It also has two real strengths worth knowing: a shorter-than-usual wait for pre-existing conditions, and a renewal guarantee with no upper age cutoff.

What actually costs you money

Five clauses from the policy wording. Two are severe enough on their own to change the outcome of a claim; the rest compound on top of them.

Room rent cap, with proportionate deduction

Critical

Room rent limits are tiered tightly by Sum Insured — as low as 1% of SI per day at the lowest tiers. Exceed your tier and the insurer proportionately cuts nearly every other line item on the bill, not just the room charge.

you have a ₹10L policy with a ₹6,000/day room limit and take a ₹9,000 room, the insurer reduces your ₹2L surgical bill by roughly 33% because of the room breach alone.

Estimated exposure: 25–40% of the total bill, out of pocket.

Verified against the wording

Expenses relating to the hospitalization will be considered in proportion to the room rent limit stated in the policy or actuals whichever is less.

Hard percentage caps on surgeon and theatre fees

CriticalOutside numbered taxonomy — flagged for financial impact

Independently of the room-rent clause, the policy caps what it will pay for doctors and surgeons at 25% of your Sum Insured, and everything else in the operating theatre — anaesthesia, medicines, implants, diagnostics — at 50%. These ceilings apply per hospitalization, regardless of what actually happened to your room.

you have a ₹5L policy and a specialist surgeon charges ₹2L, the 25%-of-SI cap limits the payout to ₹1.25L, leaving ₹75,000 for you to cover — even though your total Sum Insured was never exhausted.

Estimated exposure: ₹50,000–2L, if specialist fees or theatre costs run high.

Verified against the wording

Surgeon, Anaesthetist, Medical Practitioner, Consultants, Specialist Fees subject to a maximum of 25% of the Sum Insured per hospitalization.

Mandatory 30% co-payment on every claim

High

Not tied to entry age or an optional discount — this co-pay applies to all claims, for everyone on the policy, on top of whatever the room-rent and category caps have already deducted.

your approved claim is ₹5L, the insurer pays 70% — ₹3.5L — and you cover the remaining ₹1.5L from your own savings, before any other cap is even applied.

Estimated exposure: 30% of every approved claim, permanently.

Verified against the wording

Co-payment: This policy is subject to co-payment of 30% for all claims.

Major-disease treatment capped well below real cost

High

Cancer, cardiovascular disease, renal disease, and long-bone fractures each carry a hard rupee ceiling per policy period, scaled to Sum Insured but nowhere close to it — from ₹75,000 at the lowest tier to ₹5L at the highest.

you have a ₹10L policy and cardiovascular treatment costs ₹6L, the disease-specific cap limits the payout to ₹2.75L, leaving ₹3.25L for you to cover.

Estimated exposure: ₹2L–5L, for major-disease treatment.

Verified against the wording

The expenses payable during the entire policy period for treatment of the following diseases / conditions... is limited to the amount mentioned in table below.

Coverage > Section J

Post-hospitalization limited to 7% of the bill, then hard-capped

High

Most policies cover 60–90 days of post-discharge costs in full. This one pays 7% of your hospitalization expenses — then applies a hard rupee ceiling on top of that percentage, as low as ₹5,000 at the lowest SI tiers.

post-discharge physiotherapy and medication cost ₹40,000, the 7% calculation plus the hard cap limits your payout to around ₹7,000, leaving roughly ₹33,000 for you to cover.

Estimated exposure: ₹30,000–50,000, for recovery-period care.

Verified against the wording

Post Hospitalization medical expenses equivalent to 7% of the hospitalization expenses... subject to a maximum as per the table given below.

Why this matters: the 30% co-pay is calculated on top of whatever these other caps have already reduced — it isn't 30% of the original bill, it's 30% of what's left after room-rent and category caps have already been applied. The effective out-of-pocket share on a complex claim is routinely higher than any single clause suggests on its own.

What this gets right

For a policy this strict on payouts, two structural choices stand out as genuinely senior-friendly.

✓

Pre-existing disease wait is just 12 months — a third of the 36-month standard most policies use, which matters more for a senior-entry product than almost anything else on this page.

✓

Guaranteed lifetime renewal. Once accepted, there's no upper age cutoff that forces you off the policy later.

✓

No mandatory aggregate deductible — unlike a top-up plan, this pays from the first rupee, subject to the caps above.

✓

Day-care isn't restricted to a named list. "All day care procedures" are covered, not a fixed enumerated set.

✓

No benefit-forfeiture clauses. Nothing in the base policy permanently cancels a cover after a single use.
