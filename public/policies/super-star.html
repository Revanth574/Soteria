A genuinely clean base policy — with one built-in catch and two ways to undo it yourself.

Super Star's base cover avoids nearly every classic trap: any room with no cap, no mandatory co-pay, no deductible, no disease sub-limits, unlimited restoration. One real catch is built into the base policy regardless of what you choose. Two more traps exist only if you opt into the wrong add-ons — the base policy never has them on its own.

What actually costs you money

One of these is part of the base policy no matter what you choose. The other three only apply if you opt into the cover that carries them.

"Freeze Your Age" unlocks the moment you actually use the policy

HighBase policy

Your premium is locked at your entry age — but only until your first claim, or until you turn 56, whichever comes first. This isn't an optional cover; it applies to every Super Star policyholder.

you buy the policy at 35 and file your first claim at 48, your next renewal reprices you at 48's rate — the 13 years of locked savings end in one premium jump.

Estimated impact: a 30–150% premium jump at the next renewal after your first claim, or at age 56 regardless.

Verified against the wording

Insured age is locked at entry... till a claim is incurred... or they attain the age of 56 years whichever is earlier... Post the claim is incurred, the premium charged from next renewal onwards will be as per Insured's age.

Basic Cover > 19. Freeze Your Age

Room Rent Modification reintroduces the deduction the base policy doesn't have

HighOpt-in only

The base policy covers any room, no cap, no deduction. This optional cover trades that away for a lower premium — capping your room to Single Private AC, Shared, or General Ward, and activating a proportionate deduction the moment you breach it.

you opt for a Shared Room cap to save on premium and only a Single AC room is available during an emergency, the insurer covers the room at the shared rate but cuts your ₹3L surgical bill by 40%, costing you ₹1.2L out of pocket.

Estimated exposure: 20–50% of the total bill, only if this cover was opted and the room cap is breached.

Verified against the wording

Room Rent Modification: ...Associated Medical expenses which vary based on the room occupied by the Insured Person will be considered in proportion to the room rent stated in the policy schedule or actuals whichever is less.

Optional Covers > 12. Room Rent Modification

Smart Network's 15% discount comes with a 15% co-pay trapdoor

HighOpt-in only

Opting into Smart Network gets you a premium discount for restricting yourself to a curated hospital list. Get treated anywhere outside that list — even at a perfectly good network hospital — and a 15% co-pay applies to the entire claim.

you've opted into Smart Network and need surgery at a top-tier hospital that isn't on the Smart list, a ₹10L bill leaves you paying ₹1.5L entirely out of pocket.

Estimated exposure: 15% of the total claim, only if this cover was opted and the hospital isn't on the list.

Verified against the wording

Smart Network: ...A co-payment of 15% will be applicable (over and above other co-pay, if any) on each and every claim (except in case of accident) in case if the treatment is taken in a hospital which is outside the "Smart Network" List.

Optional Covers > 1. Smart Network

Limitless Care is a one-time parachute, not a permanent feature

MediumOpt-in only

This optional cover removes the Sum Insured ceiling for a single claim in the lifetime of the policy. The moment you use it once, it's gone permanently — it cannot be re-opted at any future renewal.

you use Limitless Care for a ₹50L organ transplant at 45, the cover is permanently removed from your policy, and a second catastrophic claim at 60 is capped strictly at your base Sum Insured.

At stake: the entire benefit, permanently, after a single use.

Verified against the wording

Limitless Care: ...Once a claim has been made under this Optional Cover, the cover will cease to exist and cannot be opted again upon subsequent renewals.

Optional Covers > 19. Limitless Care

The choices that undo the base policy's cleanliness

Every one of these is opt-in. The base policy has none of these risks on its own — you would have to choose an add-on to introduce them.

The pattern: each of these is marketed as a way to save money or gain an uncapped benefit. Each one's fine print reintroduces the exact risk — a room cap, a co-pay, a forfeiture clause — that the base policy was built to avoid.
