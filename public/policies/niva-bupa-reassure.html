What actually costs you money

Two structural clauses, plus one premium mechanic that looks like a benefit until the exact moment you need it.

Room category upgrade, with proportionate deduction

High

You choose between a Single Private Room and a Sharing Room when you buy the policy. Get admitted to a room above that category, and the insurer proportionately reduces payouts across nearly every other line item — not just the room charge.

you opted for a sharing-room plan but get admitted to a single room, the insurer pays the sharing-room rate and reduces your ₹4L surgery bill by up to 40% because of the same room breach.

Estimated exposure: 20–50% of the total bill, depending on the room-rate gap.

Verified against the wording

If you choose a room category higher than the room category available under your policy or as opted by you, claim settlement/payment may be subject to proportionate deductions on eligible medical expenses, based on the tariff arrangements agreed between the Us and the Network Provider and in accordance with the Policy terms.

Annual aggregate deductible — defined in the wording, amount set by your schedule

High

The base wording defines an aggregate annual deductible as a mechanic this product supports: your total approved expenses for the year must cross a threshold before the policy pays anything. Whether your specific policy has this active — and at what amount — isn't fixed in the wording. It's set on your individual schedule.

your schedule sets a ₹3L deductible and your first claim is ₹2L, you pay all ₹2L yourself; a second ₹2L claim later only needs ₹1L more to clear the deductible, so the insurer pays the remaining ₹1L.

Exposure: 100% of bills until cleared — only if a deductible is set on your schedule.

Verified against the wording

Annual Aggregate Deductible — This is an aggregate amount in a year that is incurred by you on Expenses... which we will NOT pay. Once the total expense exceeds this amount, balance we will pay.

"Lock the Clock" unlocks — permanently — on your first claim

Medium

The policy locks your premium at your entry age as a headline feature. That lock isn't permanent: the moment your first claim is paid, it breaks for good. Every renewal after that is priced at your real, current age.

you buy the policy at 30 and file your first claim at 45, your next renewal prices you as a 46-year-old — the 15 years of locked savings end in a single premium jump.

Estimated impact: a 30–100%+ premium jump at the next renewal after your first claim.

Verified against the wording

Lock the Clock: Your age is locked at entry when you buy the policy, till a claim is paid... Post the claim is paid, the premium charged will be as per your current age and will continue to change as per the age slabs at each renewal.

"Lock the Clock" — the two states

Marketed as a single benefit. Mechanically, it's two different premium behaviors with one trigger between them.

Before your first claim

Premium locked at entry age

Every renewal is priced as if you're still the age you were when you bought the policy — for as long as you never file a successful claim.

→

After your first paid claim

Premium recalculated to your real age

The lock breaks permanently. Every renewal from here on is priced at your actual current age, and continues rising with each age slab going forward.
