A one-time payout, with four separate conditions between you and receiving it.

This is not a hospitalization policy — it pays a single lump sum if you're diagnosed with a listed critical illness, and nothing reduces that amount once you qualify. But qualifying isn't automatic. Diagnosis alone isn't enough: you also need to have waited out the initial period, survived a minimum number of days after diagnosis, and been under the age cutoff when it happened.

Different product, different risk shape. Every other page in this series analyzes an indemnity policy — one that reimburses a variable hospital bill, where caps and co-pays reduce how much you get. This is a fixed-benefit policy: once you qualify, you get the full Sum Insured, undiminished. The real risk here is entirely about whether you qualify at all.

What can cost you the entire payout

Nothing here shaves a percentage off your claim. Each of these determines whether you receive anything at all.

Coverage ends at 66 or 67 — right as your risk peaks

Critical

This policy is not lifetime renewable. There's a hard age cutoff, and once you age out, the insurer stops covering you entirely — during the exact years critical illness risk rises fastest.

you pay premiums for 15 years and the insurer doesn't renew at 67, then at 68 you suffer a stroke, you receive nothing — not a reduced payout, nothing.

At stake: the entire Sum Insured, forfeited by age alone, regardless of how long you paid premiums.

Verified against the wording

We will not cover any person above the Age 66 for an annual policy and 67 for a 2 year policy.

The policy terminates the moment it pays you once

Critical

This is a single-use policy. The instant a claim is paid, the policy ends permanently for that person — there is no coverage remaining for any future critical illness, ever.

you claim for early-stage cancer and receive the payout, the policy terminates immediately, and a heart attack three years later has no coverage behind it at all.

At stake: all future critical-illness coverage, permanently, after one paid claim.

Verified against the wording

The Policy shall automatically terminate upon the occurrence of Critical Illness, without prejudice of Our obligation to make payment, with reference to that Insured Person.

You must survive 15 or 30 days after diagnosis to get paid

HighOutside numbered taxonomy — flagged for financial impact

A confirmed, covered diagnosis is not enough on its own. The policy only pays if the insured person survives a specified number of days — 15 or 30, depending on the plan — after that diagnosis. If the illness proves rapidly fatal, the payout doesn't happen.

a massive heart attack (a covered event) leads to death in the ICU on day 12, the family receives nothing, because the survival threshold was 15 days.

At stake: the entire Sum Insured, forfeited if death occurs before the survival window closes.

Verified against the wording

The Insured Person survives for at least 15/30 days following such diagnosis.

Section 1. Benefits

90-day initial wait — three times the usual length

MediumOutside numbered taxonomy — flagged for financial impact

Most indemnity health policies use a 30-day initial wait. This one uses 90 — a full quarter with no coverage at all, unless you're renewing continuously from a prior policy year without a break.

you're diagnosed with a covered illness 70 days after buying the policy, the claim is rejected outright, since it falls inside the 90-day window.

At stake: any diagnosis in the first 3 months, denied entirely, no exceptions for severity.

Verified against the wording

A waiting period of 90 days will apply to all claims unless the Insured Person has been insured under this policy continuously and without any break in the previous Policy Year.

Section 2. Exclusions

What has to go right for this policy to pay out

All four conditions must hold. Miss any one, and the payout is zero — this is a genuine sequence, not a checklist for convenience.

1

Diagnosed with a listed critical illness

The condition must match one of the illnesses defined in the policy's benefit schedule.

AND
2

At least 90 days have passed since the policy started

Unless you're continuously renewing without a break from a prior policy year.

AND
3

You survive 15 or 30 days after diagnosis

Per the survival period stated on your specific plan.

AND
4

You were under 66 (annual) or 67 (2-year) at the time

The hard age cutoff applies at the point of the insured event, not just at renewal.

Miss any one gate, and the outcome is the same: zero payout, regardless of how the other three conditions were met.

What this gets right — for what it is

Most fields that would show up as "strengths" on an indemnity policy in this series are simply not applicable here — that's the product type, not a feature. Two things are genuine, earned positives.

✓

Once you clear all four gates, the payout is the full Sum Insured — undiminished. No room-rent link, no co-pay, no proportional deduction reduces the amount itself. Unlike every indemnity policy in this series, nothing here shrinks your payout once you qualify.

✓

Continuous renewal keeps the 90-day clock from resetting. The wording explicitly exempts policyholders who renew without a break, so maintaining the policy protects you from re-triggering the initial wait.
